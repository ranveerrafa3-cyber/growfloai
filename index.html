The real problem

You don't have a
lead problem.

You have a follow-up problem. Most contractors are already paying for leads — the money leaks in the gap between the click and the calendar. That gap is where your competitor wins the job.

01

Whoever calls first, wins

Homeowners hire the contractor who calls back first. If you're up a ladder at 2pm, you're not calling back — and by the evening that job is already sold.

02

Tire-kickers eat your evenings

Ballpark hunters, renters, people twelve months out. You burn your nights ringing people who were never going to buy, and your time is not free.

03

No-shows kill your margin

An estimate that isn't confirmed and reminded is a coin flip. You drive forty minutes, knock on the door, and nobody's home.

04

You only get paid once

Great work, no review asked for, no follow-up sent. Every past customer is a job you already paid to acquire and never sold again.

What the gap actually costs

A twelve-thousand-dollar job, gone because someone else called back in four minutes. That isn’t a marketing problem. That’s your Tuesday.

A better way

So we stopped
selling leads.

A lead is a name, a number, and a problem you now own. An estimate is a homeowner who has already been called, qualified against your criteria, booked, confirmed and reminded. We only hand over the second one.

Questions

Before you fill
anything in

If it isn't answered here, ask on the call. We'd rather tell you it isn't a fit than sell you something that isn't.

What does it cost?

It depends on your trade, your market and how hard you want to push. A roofer scaling to a second crew in Austin isn't the same build as a plumber testing one zip code. We price it on the call, once we've seen your numbers — and if it isn't going to pay for itself, we'll tell you then rather than take the money.

Am I locked into a long contract?

We work in 90-day cycles — long enough for the ads to season and the follow-up to start compounding, short enough that you're never trapped in something that isn't working. If we're not hitting the estimate target we agreed, you're free to walk at the end of the cycle.

Who pays for the ad spend?

You do, straight to the platform. It stays your ad account, your data and your audience — we don't run it through us and we don't mark it up. Our fee is separate, and it's the only thing you pay us.

What if I already run ads, or have an agency?

Then you already know the ads aren't the hard part. Most contractors who come to us have a folder full of leads nobody called back fast enough. We can run alongside what you've got or replace it — but we won't take you on just to duplicate something that's already working for you.

What actually counts as a qualified estimate?

We agree the criteria in writing before anything goes live: they own the home, they're inside your service area, it's the kind of job you actually want, their budget matches your pricing, and their timeline is one you can service. Miss any of those and they never reach your calendar.

What happens if you miss the target?

We agree a booked-estimate number up front, set against your ad budget and how fast you want to scale. If we come in under it, we keep working the account at no additional fee until we've made up the difference.

How quickly can we start?

Usually about a week from the fit call — that covers building out the ad account, wiring up the follow-up, and briefing the sales team on your criteria. First booked estimates tend to land inside the fortnight after that.

What if someone in my city signs up first?

Then the city's gone. One contractor per trade, per city — if we've already taken a roofer in your market, we won't take another one. That's the whole reason there's a form before the call instead of a booking link.
